## Overview

This paper estimates the causal effect of a regulatory increase in the minimum tick size from $0.01 to $0.05 on daily equity trading turnover for small-capitalisation stocks. The empirical strategy exploits the exogenous variation generated by the SEC's Tick Size Pilot Program (2016–2018) as a natural experiment within a difference-in-differences framework.

The analysis extends Albuquerque, Song, and Yao (2020, *Journal of Financial Economics*, 138: 700–724) by developing an ITT-consistent sample selection procedure that preserves the statistical power to detect heterogeneous treatment effects across pre-treatment spread groups.

---

## Research Questions

1. Does the regulatory tick size increase reduce trading turnover for treated stocks relative to the control group, and does the effect escalate with the intensity of the regulatory requirement across the three pilot groups?

2. Is the reduction in trading activity concentrated among stocks whose pre-treatment quoted spreads were below $0.05 — the stocks for which the new minimum tick is mechanically binding?

---

## Hypotheses

**H1 (Average Treatment Effect):** The $0.01→$0.05 tick size increase reduces daily turnover for treated stocks relative to control stocks.

**H2 (Heterogeneous Effects):** The negative effect on turnover is larger for stocks whose pre-treatment average quoted spread was below $0.05 (constrained stocks).

---

## Empirical Strategy

**Identification:** Stratified random assignment by the SEC; treatment is orthogonal to contemporaneous firm fundamentals. Firm and date fixed effects absorb time-invariant heterogeneity and aggregate market shocks.

**Baseline Specification (H1):**
```
ln(Turnover)_it = α + Σ_k β_k (Gk × TreatmentPeriod_t)
                  + Σ_k λ_k (Gk × PostPilot_t) + θ'X_it + γ_i + δ_t + ε_it
```

**Extended Specification (H2):**
```
ln(Turnover)_it = ... + Σ_k γ_k (Gk × TreatmentPeriod_t × SmallSpread_i)
                  + Σ_k φ_k (Gk × PostPilot_t × SmallSpread_i) + ...
```

**SmallSpread_i** = 1 if the firm's 6-month pre-treatment average dollar quoted spread < $0.05.

Standard errors clustered at the firm (PERMNO) level.

---

## Key Results

**H1 — Supported.** All three pilot groups exhibit statistically significant reductions in daily log turnover during the active pilot period:

| Group | Coefficient | Turnover Change | p-value |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | −0.0884 | −8.46% | 0.007 |
| G2 | −0.0662 | −6.41% | 0.065 |
| G3 | −0.0954 | −9.10% | 0.011 |

G3 (trade-at prohibition) exhibits the largest point estimate, consistent with a dose-response relationship.

**H2 — Supported.** The treatment effect is driven almost entirely by constrained stocks (pre-spread < $0.05):

| Group | Triple Interaction | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| G1 × TreatmentPeriod × SmallSpread | −0.169 | 0.031 |
| G2 × TreatmentPeriod × SmallSpread | −0.161 | 0.074 |
| G3 × TreatmentPeriod × SmallSpread | −0.187 | 0.054 |

Unconstrained stocks (pre-spread ≥ $0.05) show no significant effect in any group, confirming that the $0.05 floor drives the result by binding mechanically against prior equilibrium spreads.

Post-pilot persistence is observed for G2 and G3 constrained stocks, suggesting lasting changes in market participant behaviour beyond the regulatory window.

## Data

The analysis uses daily stock-level data from the **Center for Research in Security Prices (CRSP)**, period January 1, 2016 – April 30, 2019, matched with the SEC Tick Size Pilot assignment list.

> **Note:** The CRSP dataset and the Robinhood/RH end-of-day holdings file (`RH daily.dta`) are excluded from this repository. Both are available through institutional data subscriptions. The do-files assume Stata datasets structured consistently with the CRSP daily stock file (`permno`, `date`, `vol`, `shrout`, `bid`, `ask`, `ret`, `prc`) and the RH matched file format used in Albuquerque et al. (2020).

**Key variable construction:**
- `ln_turnover = ln(vol / shrout)` — primary dependent variable
- `SmallSpread` — binary indicator, average pre-treatment dollar spread < $0.05
- `Inv_Price = 1 / midpoint` — control for price-level mechanical effects
- `L_abs_ret` — one-day lagged absolute return (volatility control, predetermined)
